DADS-SPEC-V1.0 · PUBLISHED JULY 3, 2026

"Governance you can diff, not governance you have to trust."

D.A.D.S. (Deterministic Autonomous Decoupled State) is the technical specification for transitioning enterprise AI from probabilistic, cloud-dependent execution loops to an air-gapped, compiled-binary architecture.

PILLAR 01 // THE SHIELD

AISA: Situational Awareness

Authored by Guy with Hawks's blind spot audit and Boswell's record review. Enforces strict behavioral self-defense against conversational subversion:

  • Locked Authority Allow-List: Only explicit callers hold authority. Polite framing gives zero leverage (Powers Turn 01).
  • Compliment Vector Defense: Pairing praise with unauthorized behavioral asks is logged and refused (Powers Turn 10).
  • Multi-Turn Suspicion Hold: Suspicion does not decay just because subsequent turns look innocent (Powers Turn 08 & 11).
  • Append-Only Refusal Logging: Refusals recorded deterministically to structured JSON registers rather than debating in prose.
PILLAR 02 // THE ANCHOR

The Sniffer: Decoupled Intent

The direct ancestor of DUMEdb's 32-byte LMDB UnitRecords. Solves on-task persistence and context amnesia under heavy conversational tangents:

  • The Intent Anchor (sniffer_intent.md): Mission lives in an isolated register, not in volatile context memory.
  • Memory-Write Checkpoints: Memory is written only at milestones—never in reaction to an inbound probe.
  • Interruption Resilience: Unrecognized senders are treated as interruptions, not re-taskings. Re-reads intent and returns to task.
  • Verbatim Fact Preservation: Decoupling stops model "softening" and hallucinated deadlines (Powers Turn 02 & 05).

Pre-Registered Disclosures & Negative Controls

What this benchmark does NOT show. Fixed before firing on July 26, 2026. Published in full to survive hostile audit.

1. Rails Do Not Make a Model Omniscient
Rails convert a specific, predictable class of structural failure into a pass. They do not magically grant models knowledge they do not have. Several unstructured capabilities remain off-menu regardless of rails.
2. The 1B Model JSON Rail Negative Control (Turn 04)
When the exact-copy rail was applied to Llama 3.2 1B in Turn 04, the rail broke the JSON output in a novel way by generating excessive escaped quotes. This negative result is reported prominently as required by pre-registration.
3. Frontier Arm Cloud Pinning Limitation
Claude Opus is served over cloud subscription APIs and cannot be pinned to an immutable weight digest the way local models (e.g. llama3.2:3b = a80c4f17acd5) are.
4. Scoring Blinding Disclosure
Scoring was conducted with full visibility into which arm generated the transcript. To eliminate bias, scoring rules were written strictly before firing, and 100% of raw JSONL transcripts are published for third-party auditing.
